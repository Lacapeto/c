Innovation Programs

Seven programs covering every stage of the arc, from a first Thursday evening to an industrial placement. Each one feeds the next.

The seven programs

Stage: Build

Bootcamps

Short, intensive skills sprints.

Focused sprints that take a member from nothing to working code in one stack — embedded C, Python for data work, or web fundamentals. Each is taught by a senior member or staff mentor and runs in the evenings so it does not collide with lectures. Participants close the bootcamp by building and defending a small working project rather than sitting a written test.

Format
In-person, evening sessions
Cadence
Twice per semester
Duration
3 weeks
Intake
40 members per bootcamp
Entry
Open to all levels; no prior experience assumed for introductory tracks

You leave with

  • A working project built and defended in front of peers
  • Working fluency in one stack
  • A place on the shortlist for the next incubation cohort
Stage: Build

Hackathons

Build something real against the clock.

Time-boxed build sprints run against a problem brief drawn from campus life or the surrounding community. Teams of three to five have 48 hours to move from a blank repository to a prototype they can demonstrate. Work is judged by a mixed panel of departmental staff and visiting industry engineers, and the strongest teams are fast-tracked into incubation.

Format
Team-based, 48-hour on-site sprint
Cadence
Once per semester
Duration
48 hours
Intake
12 teams (60 members)
Entry
Teams of 3;5, with at least one member who can ship the demo

You leave with

  • A demonstrable prototype and a public pitch
  • Feedback from an industry judging panel
  • Fast-track consideration for an incubation place
Stage: Incubate

Incubation Cohorts

Structure for an idea that already works.

For teams that have validated an idea and now need structure to take it further. Across a full semester each team is given bench space in the hub, a technical mentor, and fortnightly milestone reviews that they must pass to stay in the cohort. Teams leave with a working prototype, documentation, and a defensible plan for what comes after the cohort.

Format
Semester-long, mentor-led, milestone-gated
Cadence
One cohort per academic session
Duration
16 weeks
Intake
8 teams per cohort
Entry
A validated concept, a committed team, and a completed hub project or hackathon entry

You leave with

  • A working prototype and technical documentation
  • A milestone record and a post-cohort roadmap
  • A Demo Day slot in front of staff and industry guests
Stage: Concept

Weekly Coding Sessions

The hub’s regular heartbeat.

An open, drop-in evening that runs every week the university is in session. Members work through problem sets, pair on side projects, and get unblocked by whoever in the room has already solved that problem. There is no registration and no prerequisite beyond turning up — this is the easiest first contact a new member has with the hub.

Format
Open drop-in, peer-led
Cadence
Every Thursday during semester
Duration
2 hours per session
Intake
Open — no cap
Entry
None. Bring a laptop if you have one; machines are shared if you do not

You leave with

  • Consistent practice and a habit of shipping
  • Direct access to senior members and mentors
  • The usual route into bootcamps and project teams
Stage: Production

Research Clusters

Standing groups on an open question.

Small standing groups that work alongside departmental academic staff on a defined research question, currently spanning computer vision, embedded systems, and networks. A cluster meets weekly, maintains a shared reading list, and is expected to produce something citable. Output is aimed at conference papers, strong final-year projects, and national competition entries.

Format
Staff-supervised research group
Cadence
Weekly, ongoing across the session
Duration
Continuous membership
Intake
4;6 members per cluster
Entry
300 level and above, with a demonstrated interest in the cluster’s area

You leave with

  • A conference paper or publishable technical report
  • A supervised final-year project topic
  • Competition-ready research work
Stage: All

Mentorship Tracks

Paired guidance, not ad-hoc advice.

Junior members are paired with senior students and with alumni now working in industry. Each pairing sets its own goals at the start of the semester, meets at least fortnightly, and is formally reviewed at the halfway point so that a pairing which is not working can be changed rather than quietly abandoned. Mentors are briefed and matched by focus area, not assigned at random.

Format
One-to-one pairing, reviewed at mid-semester
Cadence
Fortnightly meetings
Duration
One semester per pairing
Intake
Matched to available mentors each semester
Entry
Active hub membership and a stated focus area to be matched on

You leave with

  • A semester of structured, goal-based guidance
  • An industry or senior-student contact beyond the hub
  • A reviewed record of progress against agreed goals
Stage: Production

SIWES Placement

Support through the industrial placement.

Practical support for the mandatory Students’ Industrial Work Experience Scheme. The hub runs technical readiness sessions before placement season, helps members assemble a portfolio that shows real work rather than coursework, and connects them to partner firms who have taken hub members before. Members are also debriefed on return so the next intake gets better guidance.

Format
Readiness sessions plus placement matching
Cadence
Annually, ahead of the SIWES window
Duration
24-week placement
Intake
Matched to available partner placements
Entry
Students entering their SIWES year, with at least one completed hub project

You leave with

  • A portfolio built on real, demonstrable project work
  • An introduction to a partner firm
  • A technical readiness review before the placement starts
